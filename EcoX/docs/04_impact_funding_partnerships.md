# Eco-X Impact, Funding & Partnerships Dossier

## Purpose

This dossier defines the funding, impact and partnership logic for Eco-X.

## Impact thesis

Eco-X links ecology, water, food, training, circular inputs and evidence. Its strongest value is not single-site restoration. Its strongest value is repeatable ecological capability.

## Funding streams

| Stream | Function |
|---|---|
| Römer support | Pre-establishment and InterSol-linked development. |
| Paid packages | Private, estate, agricultural and commercial services. |
| Buy-a-block matched deployment | Paid purchase funds community/in-need deployment. |
| Service contracts | Councils, corridors, restoration and InterSol subcontracting. |
| Grants/philanthropy | Schools, poverty, disaster, restoration and public benefit. |
| Carbon/biodiversity pathways | Future qualified projects under applicable methods and rules. |
| Research partnerships | Universities, labs, schools and controlled-biosphere research. |

## Buy-a-block model

A customer-funded deployment can trigger a matched community deployment. The model must track:

- buyer order;
- matched block/system;
- recipient site;
- deployment evidence;
- training delivered;
- monitoring results;
- maintenance status.

## Market pathways

ACCU and Nature Repair pathways are potential future economic viability streams. They are not assumed. Each project must meet current method, registration, reporting, ownership, consent, monitoring and compliance rules.

## Partnership classes

- Farmers and landholders.
- Councils and regional bodies.
- Schools and education partners.
- NGOs and humanitarian partners.
- Ecologists, hydrologists and soil scientists.
- First Nations and local custodians where applicable.
- Römer / InterSol / EMASSC linked systems.
- Universities and space-habitat research partners.

## Partner rule

Eco-X should avoid low-impact third-party capture. Partnerships must align to method quality, measurable impact, local capability and transparent reporting.
